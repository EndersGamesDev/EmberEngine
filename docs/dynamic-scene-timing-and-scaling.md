# Dynamic scene timing and scaling

## 1. Decision and control boundary

**Proposed — decision.** Add one `SceneTimingController` to the scene clock owned above the scene renderer and presenter. It reads only closed latency-statistics epochs, decides whether a scene is due, and changes at most one quality lever per decision. The presenter still runs on every redraw and still owns the surface; input still reaches both consumers at their specified read points; the scene renderer still produces only a `SceneFrame`. This is the control layer implied by the presenter document's unresolved throttle re-homing: the current throttle fields and skip decision live inside `Renderer`, while the stated destination is a clock in `app.rs` above `render_scene` and `present` (`docs/presenter-architecture.md:166-174`).

**Verified — why the boundary matters.** v7 currently makes the scene-due decision inside `render_impl`, but the present pass is recorded and handed off whether or not the scene pass is due (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:597-612`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:727-800`). The adopted architecture instead makes presentation unconditional and treats the variable-rate scene as the graceful degradation point: sim is fixed, scene is budgeted, and present is display-driven (`docs/atw-first-rendering.md:35-49`). A controller below that split could suppress the very presenter whose cadence and late input it is meant to protect.

**Proposed — thesis.** Low lag is not “render as many complete worlds as possible.” It is “never make present or input wait for scene quality that the device cannot sustain.” The controller therefore spends scene pixels first and scene freshness second, while keeping present opportunity, input sampling, deterministic simulation, and network cadence outside its actuation surface.

## 2. Objectives as distributions

**Proposed — target-class discovery.** Let `T` be the foreground present-opportunity period. Start in the 60 Hz class (`T = 16.67 ms`) and, after two valid foreground epochs, classify from the fastest stable cluster of `P3(n) - P3(n-1)` samples rather than from an advertised panel rate. Snap to 60, 90, 120, or 144 Hz when the cluster lies within 10% of that period; above 144 Hz retain the measured `T`. Classification changes setpoints only: it never inserts sleeps, skips presents, or lowers rAF cadence. A resize, focus transition, visibility transition, or surface loss invalidates the discovery window because v7 can reconfigure on resize or loss and its wasm loop is rAF-driven (`crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:171-175`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:322-351`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:614-623`).

**Proposed — primary setpoints.** The controlled distributions are same-process `I0 input_arrived → P3 present_handoff` for an input causally carried by that present, and the interval `P3(n) - P3(n-1)`. `P3` is the last honest engine-visible boundary, not scanout or photon time (`docs/latency-observability.md:150-165`, `docs/latency-observability.md:187-197`). For every target class, require present-interval p95 at or below `1.25T`, present-interval p99 at or below `2T`, spread `p95 - p50` at or below `0.25T`, input-to-handoff p95 at or below `2T`, and input-to-handoff p99 at or below `3T`.

**Derived — numeric setpoints.** The table is direct multiplication of those ratios; for example the 60 Hz row uses `T = 1000 / 60 = 16.67 ms`, so `1.25T = 20.83 ms`, `2T = 33.33 ms`, and `3T = 50.00 ms`.

|Target class|`T`|Present p95|Present p99|Maximum `p95-p50`|Input→handoff p95|Input→handoff p99|Preferred scene target|
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|60 Hz|16.67 ms|20.83 ms|33.33 ms|4.17 ms|33.33 ms|50.00 ms|60 Hz|
|90 Hz|11.11 ms|13.89 ms|22.22 ms|2.78 ms|22.22 ms|33.33 ms|72 Hz|
|120 Hz|8.33 ms|10.42 ms|16.67 ms|2.08 ms|16.67 ms|25.00 ms|72 Hz|
|144 Hz|6.94 ms|8.68 ms|13.89 ms|1.74 ms|13.89 ms|20.83 ms|72 Hz|
|Above 144 Hz|measured|`1.25T`|`2T`|`0.25T`|`2T`|`3T`|72 Hz|

**Proposed — secondary setpoints.** Normal operation keeps effective scene production at or above 40 Hz and presentation-reuse age p95, measured on the CPU trace clock as `P3 - R1` for the presented `SceneFrame.seq`, no greater than `T + 25 ms`; 30 Hz and `T + 33.33 ms` are emergency floors, not quality targets. The arithmetic is one maximum scene interval plus one present opportunity. `SceneFrame.sim_time` remains a separate deterministic-world-age value and is never subtracted from `P3` (`docs/presenter-architecture.md:62`). These bounds adopt the policy's target of at least 40 Hz, approximate 30 Hz floor, and warning that animated objects judder below about 40 Hz (`docs/atw-first-rendering.md:37-41`, `docs/atw-first-rendering.md:124-133`). They are subordinate to the primary setpoints: a fresh scene is not a success if it makes present pacing miss.

**Proposed — presenter reserve.** Track `P0 acquire`, `P1 present_encode`, and `G1 present_gpu` separately and require each available p95 to remain below `0.25T`. This is a diagnostic guardrail, not a sum: CPU and GPU lanes overlap and GPU query durations are not CPU-comparable start stamps (`docs/latency-observability.md:138-148`). If `G1` alone breaches while scene work is already absent or minimal, scene degradation is not credited with a fix; the controller holds and reports `presenter_budget_unmet` because its actuators cannot shrink the full-resolution present pass.

**Derived — consistency outranks the mean.** At 60 Hz, nineteen 16.67 ms handoffs followed by one 33.33 ms handoff average `(19 × 16.67 + 33.33) / 20 = 17.50 ms`, only 5% above the ideal mean, but the player experiences a doubled interval every twentieth handoff. The p95/p99 and `p95-p50` objectives expose that repeated hitch; a mean hides it. This is why a controller may keep lower scene quality when the mean has recovered but the tail has not.

## 3. Sensors and decision cadence

**Verified — statistics substrate.** The latency design specifies 193-bucket fixed histograms, sample count, sum, minimum, maximum, overflow, unavailable counts, p50/p95/p99 extraction, five two-second epochs, and a rolling ten-second merge (`docs/latency-observability.md:179-185`). It also specifies the local boundary taxonomy `I0..I3`, `R0..R1`, `G0`, `P0..P3`, and `G1` (`docs/latency-observability.md:150-165`). The controller consumes the immutable snapshot produced by that design; it does not add a second timing system.

**Proposed — cadence.** Close statistics every two seconds. Run the controller once, immediately after a close, against the just-closed epoch plus the rolling five-epoch view. A valid decision epoch has at least 60 present-interval samples, at least 20 scene samples when scene metrics are consulted, no clock reset, and explicit values for provenance-drop, histogram-overflow, and unsupported counters. Too few samples means `insufficient`, not zero load. The epoch containing an actuation is tagged `disturbed` and cannot justify another actuation; the next complete epoch is the first attribution window.

**Proposed — exact sensor set.** These are the only statistics that may affect scene-quality decisions.

|Sensor|Statistic and cadence|Control use|If absent or invalid|
|---|---|---|---|
|`P3(n)-P3(n-1)`|p50/p95/p99 and overflow each closed epoch|Primary pacing target and target-class discovery|Hold during invalid epochs; never substitute overlay FPS|
|Causal `I0→P3`|p95/p99, count, provenance drops each epoch|Primary engine-visible input-lag target|Hold recovery when causal samples are insufficient; do not pair unrelated input and present IDs|
|Presentation-reuse age at `P3`|Same-clock `P3 - R1` p50/p95/p99 keyed by presented `SceneFrame.seq`; report deterministic `sim_time` lag separately|Freshness constraint and proof that throttling is actually re-presenting|Report unavailable until `SceneTrace.scene_submit_at` and frame identity land; never subtract `sim_time` from the trace clock|
|`R0 scene_encode`|p50/p95 each epoch|Detect a CPU-side scene bottleneck and judge whether resolution can plausibly help|Use primary outcomes only; do not invent GPU attribution|
|`G0 scene_gpu`|p50/p95/p99 after query readback|Identify fill-rate pressure and validate a `scene_scale` response|Use conservative one-step probes; never derive GPU time from CPU residuals|
|`P0`, `P1`, `G1`|p95/p99 separately|Protect presenter reserve and identify an actuator-independent present bottleneck|Keep the missing lane marked unsupported; never add CPU and GPU durations|
|Effective scene intervals|p50/p95 from completed `SceneFrame.seq` changes|Verify the cap, missed due scenes, and settling|Hold scene-Hz recovery if completion identity is missing|
|Warp demand|p95/p99 of absolute late pose delta per axis, once stage B exists|Size guard band from actual pose demand, not from load|Keep the configured safe default; never shrink from missing data|

**Verified — unavailable GPU sensing.** v7 requests `wgpu::Features::empty()` and every scene, present, and overlay render-pass descriptor has `timestamp_writes: None` (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:240-250`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:681-709`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:733-788`). The latency design makes timestamp negotiation conditional and says unsupported adapters continue with CPU metrics while counting unavailability; query pairs yield pass durations but not exact queue wait (`docs/latency-observability.md:138-148`).

**Proposed — conservative missing-GPU policy.** With `G0` unavailable, a primary p95 failure permits one `scene_scale` degradation step, followed by a full attribution epoch. If primary p95 improves by less than one histogram bucket while load remains comparable, do not keep claiming fill-rate relief: one further step may be tried only after another bad epoch, after which stage B may engage the scene-Hz ladder. With `G1` unavailable, the controller may infer only that presentation outcomes are bad, not that the present GPU pass caused them. Recovery may use healthy primary distributions, but its report continues to say GPU attribution unavailable.

**Proposed — forbidden evidence.** No decision is based on one frame, the latest value, an EMA, a mean without tails, a maximum without count, or subtraction across CPU/GPU or client/server clocks. v7's overlay FPS is an EMA with 0.95/0.05 weights and its scene age is one instantaneous native value (`crates/ember-engine/src/overlay.rs:60-97`); the network taxonomy explicitly forbids cross-machine subtraction (`docs/latency-observability.md:167-177`). Those values remain useful diagnostics, but they are not controller sensors.

## 4. Actuators and their v7 seams

### 4.1 Dynamic actuators

**Proposed — actuator contract.** An actuator exposes its current value, requested value, actuation sequence, reason, and the first undisturbed epoch eligible to judge it. Values are discrete so traces can reproduce every decision.

|Actuator|Range and granularity|When it is legal|Actuation cost|Settling rule and exact seam|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|`scene_scale`|0.50–1.00; 0.05 quantum; degrade by 0.10 and recover by 0.05|Stage A onward|Re-create color and depth for every `SceneFrame` ring slot, replace their views, and rebuild the presenter's per-slot bind groups; temporary allocation and old-target retirement can hitch|First new-size frame makes the value effective; exclude the change epoch and wait one full epoch. v7 stores the fixed value at `Renderer.scene_scale`, initializes it to 1.0, derives both target dimensions from it, and rebuilds targets plus the present bind only through resize (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:163-183`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:278-279`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:562-575`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:863-923`).|
|Scene-Hz cap|Discrete ladder 72, 60, 48, 40, 30 Hz; omit rungs above the observed present class|Production only when stage B late-pose warp and frame-relative input are operational; diagnostic only in v7 stage A|No texture recreation; changes due-time scheduling and scene-age distribution|Effective at the next due decision; exclude the change epoch and wait one full epoch. v7's native-only fields, setter, and due branch are at `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:193-201`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:538-547`, and `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:605-612`; move all three above the stages as required by `docs/presenter-architecture.md:166-174`.|
|Guard band|2°–14° total FOV widening after stage B; 0.5° quantum; default 7°; expand by 1° and shrink by 0.5°|Only with stage B projection/crop and oversized-frustum culling; never below `2 × (p99 per-side late-angle demand + 1°)`|Projection/culling update; if constant display angular density is preserved, changed tangent extent also resizes scene targets and has the same allocation/rebind cost as `scene_scale`|Expansion is effective on the next scene; shrink only after five safe epochs. v7's projection seam is `Camera` and `view_proj` (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:93-115`); stage B's contract separates display FOV from `guard_deg` (`docs/presenter-architecture.md:61`) and the existing target-size/rebind seam is `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:562-575`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:863-923`.|

**Derived — scale leverage.** Scene pixel count is proportional to `scene_scale²`: 1.00→0.90 retains `0.90² = 81%`, 1.00→0.70 retains 49%, and 1.00→0.50 retains 25%. The controller treats those as expected fill-rate directions, not promised GPU-time ratios; the weak-device oracle says 0.5 should approach a fourfold scene-pass improvement only under a fixed, fill-rate-bound pose (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:175-181`).

**Verified — why scale needs hysteresis.** The current target constructor allocates separate color and depth textures and the present bind group directly names the color view (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:863-923`). Changing scale is therefore not a uniform write. The presenter split retains the same rule: resize re-creates `SceneFrame` and rebinds it (`docs/presenter-architecture.md:142-158`).

**Proposed — guard-band ownership.** Guard width is a demand controller, not a spare load-shedding slider. It expands immediately when p99 late rotation approaches the edge and shrinks only when five rolling epochs prove excess margin. Capacity control sees the resulting target cost and may lower `scene_scale`, but it may not shrink guard width merely because `G0` is high. The adopted policy makes the guard band the correctness defense against black edge reveal, while the weak-device analysis derives the cost of widening a 70° FOV to 77° as `tan(38.5°) / tan(35°) = 1.136` per axis and `1.136² = 1.29`, or 29% more pixels when angular density is preserved (`docs/atw-first-rendering.md:73-86`, `docs/weak-device-performance.md:31-35`).

### 4.2 Fixes and configuration, not dynamic knobs

**Verified — backface culling.** v7 sets `cull_mode: None` (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:961-965`). Enabling backface culling after the required winding audit is a one-time pipeline correctness/performance fix, not a quality level: once a face is known to be back-facing, spending work on it never improves the image. It must not be toggled by the controller (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:67-79`, `docs/weak-device-performance.md:175-182`).

**Verified — frustum culling.** The corpus found no frustum/visibility rejection in the v7 workspace and requires a guard-aware culling implementation before stage B (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:77-90`, `docs/weak-device-performance.md:189-193`). Once present, it is always enabled and its frustum follows the guard band; disabling it is not a quality recovery action.

**Verified — depth store.** Scene depth is stored even though the stage-A presenter binds only color and a sampler (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:697-707`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:903-922`). Discarding until stage C reads depth is a build-time feature-state decision, then storing becomes correctness. It is not a runtime controller knob because alternating between valid and discarded depth would make frame contents depend on control history (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:112-125`, `docs/weak-device-performance.md:181-184`).

**Verified — mipmaps, allocation retention, and release profile.** Mipmap generation before web textures, retained render scratch buffers, corrected instance-vector capacity, and a size-oriented release profile are one-time implementation/build improvements, not degradable scene quality (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:127-137`, `docs/weak-device-performance.md:159-171`, `docs/weak-device-performance.md:175-193`). Their gains should be present before controller thresholds are tuned, or tuning will encode avoidable costs into policy.

**Proposed — surface DPR.** Do not make surface backing-store scale an automatic actuator in this controller. v7 multiplies canvas CSS dimensions by device pixel ratio and configures the surface to that size (`crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:222-237`); lowering it would change the present target, presenter cost, and any GPU-composited UI, whereas `scene_scale` leaves presenter-side and DOM UI crisp (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:92-110`). A product-level maximum DPR may be chosen separately after measuring the full-resolution warp tax, but it is not silently traded by the scene controller.

## 5. Controller policy

### 5.1 State and placement

**Proposed — owner.** After the presenter split, `Gfx` in `app.rs` owns `SceneTimingController` beside `gpu`, `renderer`, `presenter`, and the `SceneFrame` ring. On every redraw it performs sim/input work at their fixed points, asks the controller whether a scene is due, optionally calls `render_scene`, reads the late view, and calls `present` unconditionally. After `P3`, statistics close an epoch if due; the controller's decision is applied before the next scene-due test. This extends the exact `Gfx` and redraw ordering already prescribed for the split (`docs/presenter-architecture.md:154-158`, `docs/presenter-architecture.md:172-174`).

**Proposed — state.** Persist `target_class`, `scene_scale`, `scene_hz_cap`, `guard_deg`, `last_actuation`, `disturbed_until_epoch`, consecutive-good/bad counters, sensor availability, and a bounded decision log. The log records snapshot sequence, threshold crossed, old/new value, and why other levers were ineligible. No control state lives in `Renderer` or `Presenter`; they apply explicit requested values.

### 5.2 Dead bands, ordering, and attribution

**Proposed — bad, critical, and good epochs.** A `bad` epoch has a primary p95 above its setpoint, or the same primary p99 above its setpoint in two consecutive epochs. A `critical` epoch has primary p99 above `4T`, histogram overflow, or p95 present interval above `2T`; it may degrade after one valid epoch. A `good` epoch has both primary p95 values, present p99, and spread at or below 85% of their setpoints, no overflow or provenance drop, and every available presenter-reserve p95 below 80% of its setpoint. Values between good and bad form the dead band and hold quality.

**Proposed — spike versus sustained load.** One epoch whose p99 fails while p95, p50, counts, and overflow remain healthy is a spike: record it and hold. Repetition in the next epoch makes it sustained. Three consecutive epochs with rising `G0` or, when `G0` is unavailable, rising primary p50 and p95, are tagged `sustained_or_thermal`; the controller may continue one degradation step per valid epoch and blocks recovery until five consecutive good epochs. This distinguishes a tail incident from a downtrend without pretending the cause is literally temperature.

**Proposed — degradation order.** First apply no-loss fixes outside the controller. At runtime, reduce `scene_scale` toward 0.50. Only after it reaches 0.50 may stage B lower scene rate through the eligible ladder, preserving 40 Hz in normal operation and using 30 Hz only for a critical primary miss. Guard band is independently demand-sized and never reduced for load. If stage B is absent, exhaustion of `scene_scale` produces `quality_floor_unmet`; v7's identity blit cannot make low scene rate feel responsive because it has no pose input and the current frame/present path uses one camera (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:37-43`).

**Proposed — recovery order.** Require five consecutive good epochs in the rolling ten-second view, then undo exactly one most-recent degradation: raise scene Hz one rung before restoring scale, and restore scale by 0.05. Restart the five-good-epoch count after every recovery action. This is deliberately slower than degradation: overload can cost input/pacing immediately, while excess quality margin costs only temporarily softer or older scenery.

**Proposed — one lever at a time.** Never change resolution and scene Hz in the same epoch. Never change guard-band allocation and `scene_scale` in the same epoch. A target-recreating actuation gets one disturbed epoch and one complete attribution epoch; a clock-only actuation gets one disturbed epoch. The actuation frequency is therefore bounded at one action per two-second epoch, and target recreation is bounded more tightly by its attribution wait. If the chosen action worsens primary p95 after settlement, roll it back once, mark that direction ineffective for the current sensor-availability signature, and choose no alternative until another closed bad epoch.

**Proposed — lever interaction.** `scene_scale` targets GPU pixel cost; scene-Hz cap targets total scene work per second; guard band targets edge validity. Lowering Hz after a scale change must use the new scale's completed attribution epoch, not the pre-change `G0`. Increasing guard width may cause a scale decrease in a later epoch, but a capacity failure may never force unsafe guard width. A presenter-reserve failure with stable `G0` does not trigger repeated scene degradation. These rules prevent two levers from claiming the same improvement and prevent the controller from destroying scene quality for a bottleneck it cannot touch.

### 5.3 Startup and transients

**Proposed — native startup.** Begin at `scene_scale = 1.00`, zero guard in stage A or 7° in stage B, and the preferred scene rung for the provisional 60 Hz class. Collect two valid epochs before any recovery. A bad first epoch may degrade scale once; further changes still obey attribution. This favors quality on a target where the native rig and overlay already exist, without letting an optimistic default delay relief for more than one epoch.

**Proposed — wasm startup.** Until the minimal wasm loop in §7 lands, there is no dynamic controller: keep a declared static policy and do not describe v7's always-render behavior as adaptation. The conservative static proposal is `scene_scale = clamp(1.5 / DPR, 0.50, 1.00)` and no production scene throttle; derived examples are 1.00 at DPR 1, 0.75 at DPR 2, and 0.50 at DPR 3. The current tree instead pins scale to 1.0 and hardcodes the wasm scene pass due (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:278-279`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:605-612`).

**Proposed — resize and surface loss.** On resize, apply one surface reconfigure and one scene-ring recreation, clear class-discovery and partial histogram epochs, tag the cause `external_resize`, and wait for two complete foreground epochs before recovery. A controller-requested scale change must not masquerade as a resize. On `Lost` or `Outdated`, exclude the dropped present and the first successful recovery handoff, reset interval baselines, and retain degraded quality rather than restarting high. v7's resize already reconfigures surface, recreates scene targets, and rebinds; its loss path reconfigures and returns (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:562-576`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:614-623`).

**Proposed — focus and visibility.** Focus loss or `document.hidden` enters `Suspended`: freeze quality, close the partial epoch as invalid, stop class discovery, and do not count the long gap. On resume, reset `P3` and input interval baselines and require two fresh foreground epochs. A raw redraw gap above `5T` without an explicit visibility signal is treated as an unknown suspension transient, not as load. v7 currently clears input only on `Focused(false)`, clamps redraw `dt` to 0.1 seconds, warns on gaps above 100 ms, and drives wasm via rAF (`crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:156-175`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:258-277`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:322-351`); actual browser visibility delivery is a measurement item, not inferred from those seams.

## 6. What is never scaled

**Proposed — present and input.** Never skip a present opportunity to make room for scene quality, never reduce input event sampling, and never move the `I3` late read earlier. The adopted pipeline makes present display-driven and the late input read the source of camera responsiveness (`docs/atw-first-rendering.md:35-49`, `docs/atw-first-rendering.md:90-104`). v7 receives relative motion in the device callback before redraw work and requests the next redraw continuously (`crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:129-138`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:303-306`); those seams stay outside controller authority.

**Proposed — deterministic simulation.** Never change the authoritative simulation rate, fixed step, tick ordering, or protocol meaning. The arena authority defines `FIXED_DT = 1/60` and applies it in `Sim::step` (`crates/arena-core/src/shooter.rs:1-5`, `crates/arena-core/src/shooter.rs:349-379`). When the client-side fixed-step architecture lands, it remains equally outside the controller. Quality control decides when to render a state, not which state exists.

**Proposed — network cadence.** Never change intent-send, snapshot, acknowledgement, ping, or server tick cadence in response to graphics load. The current shooter constructs input every 0.05 seconds of client game time and sends the held movement/aim state plus sequence and view tick (`crates/arena/src/online.rs:693-726`); network metrics have their own `C0..C4` and `S0..S5` taxonomy (`docs/latency-observability.md:167-177`). They may explain lag, but they do not actuate scene quality.

**Verified — the prediction trap and dependency.** The shipped arena client does not yet have a fixed-step accumulator: it advances time and predicts `move_circle` with render `dt`, while the server uses `FIXED_DT` (`crates/arena/src/online.rs:403-409`, `crates/arena/src/online.rs:729-743`, `crates/arena-core/src/shooter.rs:349-379`). Collision resolves X then Z from one destination sample, so results depend on step subdivision (`crates/arena-core/src/shooter.rs:185-217`). The weak-device analysis identifies increasing reconciliation divergence as frame rate falls (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:139-157`).

**Proposed — dependency rule.** Production scene-Hz scaling must not alter the cadence or `dt` supplied to `ShooterGame::update`. The presenter split must first separate redraw/update from scene due, and the prediction path must either run at its own fixed cadence or receive a render-independent integration step. Until that dependency is verified, only resolution may adapt in the shipped arena; using scene throttle to reduce update frequency would trade visual load for simulation divergence.

## 7. Per-target policy

**Verified — native capability.** Native v7 has the scene-Hz fields and setter, scene-age readout, egui throttle, and `web_time::Instant`, but it has no histogram controller and no GPU timestamps (`crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:193-201`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:538-547`, `crates/ember-engine/src/overlay.rs:20-49`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:1-3`, `docs/latency-observability.md:118-122`). After observability and the presenter split land, native enables CPU sensors, conditional `G0/G1`, dynamic `scene_scale`, and—only at stage B with render-independent prediction—the scene-Hz ladder. The overlay displays controller state but does not own it.

**Derived — wasm capability today.** The overlay module and throttle state are native-only, wasm hardcodes every scene pass due, and the plain render branch has no control input (`crates/ember-engine/src/lib.rs:9-18`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:193-201`, `crates/ember-engine/src/renderer.rs:605-612`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:282-302`). Wasm does have a Performance-backed `web_time::Instant`, rAF-driven redraw, target recreation on backing-size change, and a WebGL fallback (`crates/ember-engine/Cargo.toml:13-15`, `crates/ember-engine/Cargo.toml:28-35`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:222-237`, `crates/ember-engine/src/app.rs:322-351`). Those verified primitives are sufficient for a minimal CPU-outcome loop, but v7 does not assemble them.

**Proposed — minimal wasm loop.** Make histogram collection and `SceneTimingController` target-neutral; move the scene clock above both stages; add the same target-recreation setter used natively; feed `P3` interval, causal `I0→P3`, `R0`, scene age, counts, and explicit GPU-unavailable state; export an immutable snapshot as JSON from the wasm API rather than requiring egui. Add explicit `visibilitychange` input or a verified winit equivalent. This completes the corpus proposal to keep metrics target-neutral and expose wasm snapshots without mutating the frozen v7 page (`docs/latency-observability.md:199-205`).

**Proposed — wasm fallback.** If browser clock quantum makes the primary setpoint finer than two observed quanta, report the coarse interval and hold recovery; if GPU queries are unsupported, follow the CPU/primary conservative policy in §3; if visibility is unknown after a long rAF gap, invalidate rather than degrade. No sensor is replaced with a guessed number. Browser timer precision and post-handoff compositor time remain explicitly unavailable (`docs/latency-observability.md:193-197`).

## 8. Testable oracles

**Proposed — O1, deterministic policy replay.** Feed a pure controller a recorded sequence of epoch snapshots twice and require byte-identical decision logs. No decision may refer to wall time outside the snapshot epoch ID, and unsupported sensors must remain unsupported. This proves policy reproducibility without a GPU.

**Proposed — O2, convergence under imposed scene load.** Begin at full quality, inject a stable scene-only load whose healthy setting is known, and require the first scale degradation within one closed bad epoch. Let `N` be the number of required target-recreating degradation actions; primary p95 must return inside its setpoint no later than `4N + 2` seconds after the first complete overloaded epoch, because each action consumes one disturbed two-second epoch and one attribution epoch, unless M2 establishes a larger declared settling bound. The final state must be the highest tested quality whose next-higher probe failed, not merely the minimum.

**Proposed — O3, no oscillation.** Under a stationary load within the dead band, the decision log contains zero actuation. Under noise alternating between 84% and 99% of setpoint, it also contains zero actuation. Across any trace, total actuation is at most one per closed two-second epoch, no epoch changes two levers, and target recreation obeys its extra attribution wait. A 120-second stationary run must not alternate between adjacent settings.

**Proposed — O4, recovery.** Remove imposed load after convergence. Quality must not rise for the first four good epochs, must rise by exactly one reverse-order step after the fifth, and must restart the five-epoch qualification after that action. Primary p95/p99 may not leave their setpoints during recovery; if they do, the probe rolls back and recovery stops.

**Proposed — O5, transient immunity.** A scripted resize, focus loss/resume, surface-loss injection, and wasm visibility pause produces invalidated epochs and zero degradation decisions. The first post-resume interval is not inserted into the pacing histogram, and pre-pause tail samples do not authorize recovery.

**Proposed — O6, perceived-quality claim.** At the same imposed load, compare controller disabled at full quality with controller enabled. The claim is accepted only if enabled p95 present interval and p95 input→handoff meet the class setpoints, present sample count does not fall, and scene age stays within the selected normal or emergency bound. Report the resulting scale and scene rate beside the distributions. A better mean with unchanged or worse p95 is a failure.

**Proposed — O7, stage-A safety.** Against v7's identity presenter, force overload beyond `scene_scale = 0.50` and require `quality_floor_unmet` with no production scene-Hz reduction. Repeat after stage B and render-independent prediction are enabled; only then may the cap ladder engage while present count remains unchanged. This oracle prevents a debug throttle from silently becoming a camera-latency policy.

## 10. Landing order and coordination items

**Proposed — landing order.** First land latency epochs and target-neutral snapshots. Second complete the presenter split and move the scene clock into `Gfx`. Third add `scene_scale` recreation as the only production actuator and run M1/M2. Fourth land stage B, guard-demand sensing, and the input/prediction dependency. Fifth enable the scene-Hz ladder on both targets. Static performance fixes should precede threshold tuning but do not need to share commits with the controller.

**Proposed — upstream coordination.** The sharp decisions are: who owns the future `Gfx` type while `app.rs` is private; whether ring slots are recreated together or drained and replaced incrementally; how GPU completion marks a target safe to retire; whether guard pixels enlarge target dimensions or trade angular density inside fixed dimensions; which browser event is authoritative for visibility; and whether arena prediction becomes fixed-step or merely render-independent before production throttling. Each changes an actuation seam or settling rule and must be resolved before thresholds become compatibility promises.

## 11. Relationship to the corpus

**Proposed — adopted policy completion.** This document does not supersede the ATW-first decision. It operationalizes its three clocks, makes “dynamic resolution is free” a bounded actuator, and turns the debug scene-Hz throttle into an epoch-driven production clock only after the warp can preserve camera response (`docs/atw-first-rendering.md:35-65`, `docs/atw-first-rendering.md:112-122`). Present ownership, late input, and the rule that UI remains presenter-side are unchanged.

**Proposed — presenter completion.** This document resolves the policy half of presenter architecture §8.2: the throttle moves above both stages because the same owner must schedule optional scene work and unconditional presentation. It does not replace the `SceneFrame` contract, ring completion rules, module split, or shader-reload coordination (`docs/presenter-architecture.md:26-69`, `docs/presenter-architecture.md:126-174`).

**Proposed — observability consumer.** Latency observability remains the sensor plan and vocabulary. This document consumes its fixed epochs, histograms, causal IDs, honest `P3` endpoint, and unsupported counts; it adds setpoints and policy but does not redefine timestamps or claim scanout knowledge (`docs/latency-observability.md:124-205`). The `C0..C4` and `S0..S5` network fork remains observable and explicitly non-actuating.

**Proposed — weak-device completion.** This document completes lever 1 by specifying `scene_scale` range, recreation semantics, hysteresis, and recovery; completes lever 4 by making the scene clock target-neutral and defining the minimal wasm loop; classifies backface/frustum culling, depth store, mipmaps, retained buffers, and release profile as one-time fixes rather than controller settings; and carries forward the render-`dt` prediction dependency as a gate on scene-Hz actuation (`docs/weak-device-performance.md:175-193`, `docs/weak-device-performance.md:139-157`).

**Proposed — input boundary.** The input-latch design remains authoritative for two independent consumers and the frame-carried `input_mark`. The controller reads only timing/provenance summaries and never advances a mark, changes a delta, or lets the cosmetic view path author simulation or network state (`docs/input-latch.md:50-110`, `docs/input-latch.md:114-142`).
