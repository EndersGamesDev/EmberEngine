# One dimension per job: genuine content in a compact world

Every construct introduced here is proposed. Names describe contracts rather than APIs or types.

## 1. Ruling: the internal dimension is double-booked

The 4D-first engine promises authoritative higher-dimensional solids, changing three-dimensional cross-sections, phase movement, and collisions outside the visible slice (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:5-7`, `docs/4d-first-engine.md:79-83`, `docs/4d-first-engine.md:120-122`). The compact torus world protects ordinary comprehension by placing intact 3D scenery instead of slicing it, then couples an actor's transverse support to the same near-period spectrum that governs recurrence and self-sight (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:23`, `docs/4d-torus-world.md:98-104`). Its published cubic example limits transverse half-support to `0.254 m` for self-sight beyond `500 m` and `0.0564 m` beyond `1 km` at `L = 128 m`, while its planning law makes image distance proportional to `L^(4/3) r_perp^(-1/3)` (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:108-121`).

That is one internal dimension doing two incompatible jobs. World topology needs small internal support to protect recurrence, injectivity, sightline, and self-image margins; genuine content needs meter-scale internal support so changing sections, extended interiors, and player phase have useful range. The adopted compact world resolves the collision by reserving sliced behavior for exceptional actors and making the environment intact and 3D, so its endless world succeeds by evicting most of the content that motivated 4D-first authority (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:23`, `docs/4d-torus-world.md:98-100`).

The ruling is one dimension per job. Ambient space is `R^5`, the quotient is a flat rank-5 torus, the physical leaf remains three-dimensional, and its two-dimensional internal complement separates a world circle from a content circle. Ordinary placed scenery remains intact and 3D; only genuine content actors and interiors occupy the content circle.

This ruling explicitly supersedes the adopted rejection of a 3D leaf in five spatial dimensions. That rejection correctly found that a better thickness exponent alone did not justify ten rotational planes, 2D windows, and 5D source assets; content occupancy was absent from that comparison, and once it is charged, the machinery becomes necessary while the previously computed exponent improvement becomes a windfall rather than the motive (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:108-119`).

## 2. Content classes and separate certificates

The reference content profile reserves a content-circle half-range `R_c = 1.28 m`, hence a `2.56 m` full local-chart span, while leaving the world-circle support limit to its own exhaustive gate. This is a declared design requirement, not a geometric constant: `1.28 m` is chosen because it is unambiguously meter-scale and permits direct comparison with the adopted spectrum's illustrative actor (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:108-121`). A title may declare a larger `R_c`, but doing so enlarges every swept-support and image search below; a profile with less than `1 m` of full usable span has not demonstrated the content need that justifies rank five.

|Content class|Transverse extent required by the reference profile|Admission certificate|What it may never borrow|
|---|---|---|---|
|Free higher-dimensional actor|Its authored content-axis half-support may reach `1.28 m`, and its visible 3D section must change materially over at least `1 m` of content-relative motion|Exhaustive rank-5 image and self-overlap search over the whole allowed `SO(5)` orientation envelope, plus bounded slice-cell, contact, and image-count ceilings|A favorable content-axis miss may not excuse a failed world-circle recurrence, self-sight, injectivity, or interaction margin|
|Content-extended interior|Its connected authored interior must expose at least `2.56 m` of usable content-axis span behind an intact 3D mouth or hull|Exhaustive search over the union of interior support, doorway transition, player swept support, and interaction halo, plus a continuous reachability proof in the authored 5D complex|The exterior remains an intact placed 3D affordance, and interior occupancy may neither widen an ordinary placement window nor move a world-circle phase boundary|
|Player content phase|The authoritative offset is clamped to `[-1.28 m,+1.28 m]` around an explicit phase anchor and never wraps merely because the underlying coordinate is a circle|Exhaustive rank-5 image/support enumeration over the player collider swept through the full interval, fixed-tick collision tests, and playtests of route prediction and unseen contact|The ability holds world-circle phase fixed and cannot spend recurrence margin, suppress an image, bypass a window transition, or acquire a lifetime winding coordinate|

A free actor is a genuine 5D solid whose intersection with the physical 3-plane swells, shrinks, splits, merges, appears, or disappears as its pose or the content phase changes. “Materially” in its admission row is an asserted playtest threshold that each product must replace with a measured silhouette, volume, or affordance threshold; the geometric certificate proves correctness and bounded cost, while the playtest proves that the extra dimension is perceptible rather than decorative. A fully free `SO(5)` pose can rotate content extent toward the world axis, so the support certificate computes the maximum world-axis projection over every allowed orientation; a failing asset is orientation-constrained or rejected, never waved through because its unrotated pose was thin.

A content-extended interior is not a teleport and not a larger 3D room hidden by culling. Its placed 3D mouth enters a connected 5D volume; inside the certified transition zone, bounded content-phase motion carries the player's 3D section continuously through authored chambers at different content coordinates, and collision follows the same swept 5D path. The `2.56 m` reference span is the arithmetic result `2 R_c`, not a claim that it guarantees a particular room count; authoring must show that the admitted span produces more usable volume than the intact 3D hull communicates.

Player content phase is a local ability, not an accumulated universal-cover coordinate. The phase anchor is canonical quotient state; changing it is allowed only at an authored transition whose certificate includes the old interval, the new interval, and the continuous connecting sweep, so repeated transitions cannot smuggle in an uncertified unbounded range. Speed, acceleration, cooldown, and cueing are content parameters, but the range, swept collision support, image ceiling, and fixed-tick sampling are session-manifest facts.

All three classes carry two internal support records, not one scalar renamed twice. The world record is evaluated against every adopted recurrence, sightline, self-image, placement, physics, networking, and weak-target gate; the content record is evaluated against a separate occupancy range, first-image horizon, phase sweep, and authoring budget. Passing one record never relaxes the other, and a joint 2D image search is required because a rank-5 lattice vector can be harmless in either component alone yet enter the body's full internal support region.

## 3. Rank-5 quotient and two internal circles

Let `A` be an invertible `5 x 5` real matrix, let `Lambda = A Z^5`, and let `T = R^5 / Lambda`. The physical space `E` is still a 3-plane, its orthogonal internal complement `U` is a 2-plane, and orthogonal projections are `pi_E` and `pi_U`. The rank-4 construction's lattice, irrational leaf, injective projection, and density tests generalize by dimension counting to `E intersect Lambda = {0}`, `U intersect Lambda = {0}`, and `Lambda* intersect U = {0}`; these are new rank-5 obligations, not conclusions inherited from the adopted rank-4 checks (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:27-35`).

The manifest gives `U` an oriented orthonormal chart `(e_u,e_v)`. The `u` coordinate is the world-circle direction and owns compactness, placement recurrence, sightline, and world self-image policy; the `v` coordinate is the content-circle direction and owns genuine-content occupancy, player content phase, and its additional self-image policy. Both are compact phases because they live in `T`, but “world axis” and “content axis” mean shortest signed coordinates in a chosen local chart, never unbounded coordinates in the universal cover; their chart periods and transitions are manifest data.

The two circles are deliberately not exchangeable. Swapping or rotating `(e_u,e_v)` changes which support and windows are charged to each certificate, so it changes session identity and forces every affected enumeration again. The content-circle period is a design lever: in an aligned toy circle, swept half-support `R_s` requires period `P_v > 2 R_s` to avoid touching the immediately translated image, derived by requiring two diameter intervals not to overlap; a general skew rank-5 lattice receives no such shortcut and passes only the exhaustive image search.

For source record `j` with quotient phase `s_j`, leaf offset `o`, and bounded acceptance region `W_j` in a chosen lift of the internal chart, placed centers are `P_j = { pi_E(s_j + A k - o) | k in Z^5 and pi_U(s_j + A k - o) in W_j }`. This is the rank-5 rederivation of the adopted rank-4 placement equation, whose acceptance set was a half-open interval (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:27-41`). `W_j` is a finite union of bounded piecewise-convex 2D cells with deterministic edge predicates; a region that crosses a chart seam is stored as finite seam-split cells, not tested through an ambiguous wrapped polygon.

Ordinary placed scenery uses a world interval crossed with the entire content circle. It therefore has no content edge, remains intact and 3D, and cannot appear, disappear, or change collider merely because the player uses content phase; this preserves the adopted placement ruling and reserves marginal intersections for explicit content (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:23`, `docs/4d-torus-world.md:98-100`). A genuine-content record may use a proper 2D region, but every resulting content-edge transition is authoritative, fixed-tick, staged, and charged to that record's phase and comprehension gates.

The canonical quotient representative uses five lattice coordinates: compute `a = inverse(A) p`, reduce every component to `frac(a_i)` in `[0,1)`, and store `q = A frac(a)`. Exact canonical faces use the adopted lower-inclusive, upper-exclusive ownership independently in all five components, while closest-image ties extend the adopted lexicographic rule to `(squared_distance,n_0,n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)` (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:72-86`). Five-component canonicalization and image labels are rank-5 definitions and require new proofs even though their form resembles the rank-4 definitions.

Window ownership extends the same half-open rule by one symbolic perturbation. For exact internal point `z`, membership is the ordinary interior membership of `z + epsilon e_u + epsilon^2 e_v` in the limit as positive `epsilon` tends to zero: the `u` sign owns a generic edge, the `v` sign breaks a boundary tangent to `e_u`, and their ordered pair selects exactly one cell at a corner of a declared partition. On an axis-aligned rectangle this reduces to `[u_min,u_max) x [v_min,v_max)`; adaptive exact signs evaluate the first nonzero coefficient, so no numerical epsilon is stored or tuned. Intentional overlap between different motif records remains multiple selection, while an exclusive partition must prove that this edge-and-corner rule gives exactly one owner everywhere.

Site density is rederived from a large physical 3-volume crossed with internal area: `rho_j = area(W_j) / abs(det(A))`. The numerator has square-length units, the covolume has fifth-power length units, and the quotient therefore has inverse-cubic units as a physical site density must; this dimensional check is also why a 1D window formula cannot be reused. Exact populations, candidate ceilings, recurrence, and self-images remain enumeration results rather than consequences of this asymptotic density.

## 4. Two phase inputs and the unchanged ATW boundary

The visible slice is an oriented affine 3-plane in `R^5` with two oriented normals `(n_u,n_v)` spanning `U` and two signed offsets `(h_u,h_v)`. Ordinary locomotion and look remain inside the slice. The two normal translations are distinct fixed-tick gameplay inputs with distinct cumulative latch marks, frame stamps, rate limits, observability counters, and authority checks.

Player content phase changes only `h_v`, is clamped to the certified interval around its phase anchor, and leaves `h_u` fixed. It changes cross-sections of genuine actors, advances the continuous path through content-extended interiors, and may cross proper content-window edges, but ordinary scenery has no such edge because its window spans the content circle. The adopted engine's single normal phase input therefore maps to this bounded content control when its purpose is the player's higher-dimensional ability; its prior fixed-tick and noncosmetic status remains unchanged (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:73-85`, `docs/4d-first-engine.md:112-118`).

World-circle phase changes only `h_u` and leaves `h_v` fixed. It remains an explicit world mechanic that can change placed-site membership, recurrence phase, quotient images, colliders, navigation evidence, and source queries, with the adopted one-sided transitions and materialization hazards intact (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:37-43`, `docs/4d-torus-world.md:161-173`, `docs/4d-torus-world.md:191-206`). It is never an alternate binding for content phase and never a way to exceed the content ability's clamp.

Nothing about two normals improves asynchronous timewarp. A completed image contains color and depth only for the old 3-plane; changing either `h_u` or `h_v` asks for points absent from that plane, so both inputs require authoritative simulation, affected placement queries, codimension-two slicing, and a scene render. The adopted categorical boundary already rejects normal motion, tilt, finite neighboring slices, and best-effort 3D approximation; it applies independently to each normal here (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:87-118`, `docs/4d-torus-world.md:167-173`).

A level-4 sample is consequently stamped with the ordered normal frame, both offsets, both phase-input marks, source-manifest identity, world tick, slice ID, and scene sequence. The presenter may apply only a within-plane `SO(3)` correction or the already-adopted bounded depth reprojection inside that exact plane; a mismatch in either phase mark or offset is `reslice_required`, not a blend between circles. Plane tilt in `R^5` has six slice-changing degrees of freedom, computed as `3 x 2` choices mixing a physical basis direction with an internal normal, and every enabled component follows the same non-warpable path.

## 5. Machinery forced by genuine content

This is a 5D generalization motivated entirely by content. Compactness alone did not justify it, exactly as the adopted rejection concluded; actors, interiors, and player occupancy across a second internal coordinate now force every row below (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:108-121`). Removing a row while retaining its forcing class would make that class cosmetic or incorrect.

|Required machinery|Content class that forces it|Irreducible cost|
|---|---|---|
|Rank-5 lattice, five-component canonical coordinates, lift labels, and closest-image searches|Every class, because each has authoritative position or support on the content circle|One more integer-search dimension, larger candidate sets, five exact face predicates, and wider image/contact keys|
|Bounded 2D acceptance windows with edge and corner ownership|Content-bearing placed records and content-extended interiors|Area predicates, seam splits, corner degeneracies, and 2D window enumeration replace interval tests|
|Codimension-two slice kernel for a 3-plane in `R^5`|Changing-section actors, interior traversal, and content phase|A new topology table, exact two-expression predicates, higher-dimensional acceleration, and new crack/degeneracy oracles|
|Validated 5D solids and authoring tools|Free actors and content-extended interiors|5-simplex fills, 4-simplex boundary cells, 4-manifold inspection, 2D slice preview, repair, LOD, and materially larger cooked assets|
|Full `SO(5)` rigid dynamics|Free actors|Ten rotational planes, 10-component angular state, 10 x 10 inertia, wider Jacobians, and larger contact regions|
|Five-component quotient pose and an `SO(5)` orientation codec|Every networked moving content body|More position bits, no Spin(4) quaternion-pair shortcut, new canonicalization, interpolation, error, loss, and rollback proofs|
|2D internal support certificates|Every class|Orientation-swept support regions and exhaustive image tests replace one transverse half-width|

The slicing change is not a mechanical extension of the adopted one-sign hyperplane slicer. That kernel intersects a tetrahedral boundary cell with one affine equation, classifies one sign per vertex, and obtains output vertices on crossing edges (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:33-53`). Here a 4-simplex boundary cell meets two affine equations; the generic intersection dimension is `4 + 3 - 5 = 2`, and polygon vertices can lie in the cell's triangular 2-faces rather than on its edges. The proposed kernel must solve the two constraints in canonical barycentric coordinates, cache shared face intersections by stable topology ID, define exact rank-deficient and tangent cases, orient the resulting 2D surface inside the visible 3-plane, and prove that adjacent 4-simplices emit identical boundaries.

A genuine source solid is a conforming 5D volume filled by 5-simplices and bounded by oriented 4-simplices. Its visible result is still ordinary 3D render geometry—the codimension-two intersection of its 4D boundary with the 3-plane is a 2D surface—but authoring must reason about a 4-manifold boundary and a 5D interior. Extruding an adopted 4D asset through content extent can seed fixtures, yet arbitrary free actors and interiors require new 5D primitives, sweeps, manifold validation, support maps, mass integration, and content-phase previews; calling an old 4D asset “5D” after adding a zero coordinate would repeat the zero-thickness error already rejected for 3D-to-4D migration (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:21-31`, `docs/4d-first-engine.md:226-232`).

`SO(5)` has `5(5-1)/2 = 10` rotation planes. Angular velocity, momentum, and torque are 10-component bivectors; inertia is a mass-derived symmetric positive-definite operator on that space, represented by a `10 x 10` matrix, and a rigid body's generalized velocity has `5 + 10 = 15` components. Two interpenetrating 5D bodies have 4D boundaries whose generic intersection is three-dimensional because `4 + 4 - 5 = 3`; a contact hyperplane has four tangent friction directions. These counts are derived by the same dimension formulas used in the adopted 4D dynamics, but the adopted six-plane, `6 x 6` inertia, ten-component generalized velocity, and 0D-to-3D contact machinery are evidence for rederivation, not reusable certificates (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:120-168`).

The pose codec widens honestly. Reusing the adopted 24-bit fractional policy gives five position components, hence `5 x 24 / 8 = 15` bytes, with Euclidean position error at most `norm(A)_2 sqrt(5) / 2^25` by applying the operator norm to five component errors each bounded by half a quantum. There is no Spin(4) pair for a general `SO(5)` orientation, so the proposed correctness-first oracle codec carries all 25 entries of a validated `5 x 5` `f32` rotation matrix: 100 orientation bytes and 115 quotient-pose bytes total, versus the adopted proposed rank-4 quotient pose's 12 position bytes plus 13 orientation bytes (`docs/4d-torus-world.md:143-149`). This overcomplete wire baseline does not prescribe the runtime orientation representation; a smaller production codec must define a global canonical factorization, interpolation lift, quantization bound, singularity behavior, and exhaustive error search before claiming savings.

The 2D support artifact is a bounded region in the internal chart for every certified pose, not the pair of maxima alone. Actor and player certificates conservatively union those regions over their orientation and phase envelopes; interior certificates union every occupied cell, doorway sweep, and participant halo. Enumeration tests lattice translations against the full region first, then evaluates physical image distance, view, collision, and candidate ceilings, because a bounding rectangle may reject safe content but may never certify an unsafe corner as absent.

## 6. Rank-generic engineering, dimension-specific proof

Ambient rank becomes an immutable session-manifest parameter rather than an engine axiom. The certified set begins with ranks three, four, and five: rank three supplies the direct-quotient and conventional-physics laboratories, rank four supplies the adopted one-internal-dimension world, and rank five supplies the two-job content world. A snapshot, replay, asset, cooker certificate, or connection whose rank differs from the manifest is incompatible input; rank never changes inside a running session.

This ruling follows from verification work the corpus already requires. The 4D-first migration keeps constrained 3D simulation as the exact invariant-subspace and rollback oracle before enabling phase and full rotation, while the torus-world migration runs a rational rank-3 quotient laboratory before compact rank-4 authority (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:226-244`, `docs/4d-torus-world.md:210-225`). Geometry, lattice, canonicalization, closest-image, support, dynamics, wire, and replay kernels therefore already need more than one rank, and lower ranks are the only complete visual and analytic oracles that rank-four and rank-five results will ever have.

Rank-generic does not mean “replace a constant and trust the compiler.” Shared linear algebra, lattice traversal, stable ordering, manifest validation, and test generation accept rank as a parameter, while topology tables, degeneracy rules, rotation representations, inertia layouts, contact reduction, windows, and codecs remain explicit rank-specific modules or data. The codimension-two kernel in section 5 is the sharp example: it can share predicates and IDs with the adopted slicer but cannot share the adopted sign-mask proof (`docs/4d-first-engine.md:33-53`).

|Manifest rank|Required oracle role|Certification that remains local to that rank|
|---|---|---|
|3|Direct quotient, ordinary rigid-body behavior, canonical-face visibility, and exact-repeat diagnostics|Three-component images and positions, 3D contact and rotation, rank-3 closest images, wire bounds, and quotient spectra|
|4|Constrained-3D comparison, one-sign hyperplane slicing, Spin(4) dynamics, irrational-leaf placement, and the adopted near-period staircase|Four-component images and positions, interval windows, six-plane rotation, rank-4 spectra, self-images, and the 25-byte proposed pose|
|5|Rank-4 embeddings, analytic simplex sections, product-window fixtures, and independently enumerated small lattices|Five-component images and positions, 2D windows, codimension-two slicing, ten-plane rotation, 2D support, rank-5 spectra, and the widened pose|

No certification transfers across dimensions by renaming. Every dimension-specific proof, bound, exhaustive search radius, example parameter, degeneracy case, codec width and error, solver budget, weak-target floor, and perceptual threshold is rederived and stored with its rank; a passing lower-rank oracle can expose a contradiction but cannot certify a higher-rank session. Conversely, a successful rank-five profile does not amend the adopted rank-four numbers or make their rejection gates optional.
